"Why do I need a website if I already have Instagram?" It's one of the questions I hear most, and it's a fair one: if customers already message you on social media, a website looks like an extra expense. The short answer is that **they don't compete, they complement each other.** The long answer has to do with a risk almost nobody sees until it happens to them.

## What social media does well

Social platforms are excellent at what they're built for:

- **Getting you discovered** by people who weren't looking for you.
- **Showing your day-to-day:** work in progress, your team, results. That builds familiarity.
- **Keeping you top of mind** with people who already know you, so they remember you when they need you.
- **Conversation:** DMs, comments, quick replies.

It's the front door. For many local businesses, most early customers arrive that way, and that's fine.

## The problem with relying only on social media

### You don't own it

Your profile lives in someone else's house. If your account gets locked by mistake, hacked, or the platform changes its rules, you lose your storefront and your contact list overnight. Recovering an account can take weeks, and during those weeks your business doesn't exist for anyone looking for you there.

### Reach depends on an algorithm

A thousand followers doesn't mean a thousand people see your post. Platforms show each post to a fraction of your audience, and more and more reach has to be bought. Your visibility depends on rules that change without notice.

### It's built to entertain, not to help people decide

People open social apps to unwind. When someone is choosing who to hire, especially for a service that costs hundreds or thousands of dollars, they want organized information: what's included, what it costs, what you've done before, how the process works. On a profile, all of that is buried under old posts.

### It doesn't get you found on Google

When someone searches "dentist in San Antonio" or "roofing contractor in Houston," Google shows the map and websites. Your Instagram profile almost never appears for that search, and that search comes from people who already have the need and are ready to hire.

## What your website does that social media can't

- **You own it.** The domain, the content and the contacts are yours, whatever platform you use.
- **It puts you on Google** when people search for what you offer, with a page per service.
- **It organizes information** for people comparing options: services, prices, process, FAQs, case studies.
- **It converts:** forms, call and text buttons, online booking, all designed to get the next step.
- **It measures:** you know how many people arrived, from where, and how many contacted you. Social shows you likes; your site shows you leads.

## The combination that sells

The strategy is simple: **social attracts, the website converts.**

1. On social, post what grabs attention: before and after, tips, your real work.
2. From the post, profile or ad, send people to a specific page on your site, not a generic homepage.
3. On that page, they find the full information and a button to contact you or book.
4. Your site records where they came from, so you know which kind of post brings customers and which only brings likes.

Social without a website is inviting people to a store that doesn't exist. A website without social is a good store on a street nobody walks down. Together, each makes the other stronger.

## What if I don't have time for both?

That's the most common situation, and the answer isn't spreading thin time across everything. A practical order:

1. **Google Business Profile**, complete. It's free and, for a local business, often brings more calls than any social network. Step by step in [how to show up on Google Maps](/en/blog/how-to-show-up-on-google-maps/).
2. **A simple website** with your services, area and contact info. Built once, it works on its own. If budget is the limit, the free options and their limits are in [how to create a website for your small business](/en/blog/free-website-for-small-business/).
3. **One social network** where your customers are, used consistently. One done well beats three abandoned.

If your business serves customers in two languages, the website matters even more: it's the only place you can have both versions done properly, each findable on Google. More in [bilingual websites in English and Spanish](/en/blog/bilingual-website-english-spanish/).
