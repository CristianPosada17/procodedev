You have a website, it looks fine, but customers aren't coming in. It's one of the most frustrating situations for any business: you invested time and money and the site feels like decoration nobody uses. The good news is that **the problem usually isn't your business; it's how the site is built.** Here are the seven most common reasons a website doesn't produce leads, and how to fix each one.

## The short answer

A website doesn't sell when it **doesn't make clear to the visitor what you do, why they should trust you and what to do next.** If someone can't understand your offer in five seconds and doesn't see an obvious way to contact you, they leave. Selling online is mostly about removing doubt and friction.

## 1. Nobody can tell what you do in the first seconds

Visitors arrive and can't tell what you sell, who it's for or what they get. If your header says something generic like "Welcome to our website," you've lost the sale.

**The fix:** at the top, one clear line about what you do and for whom, plus the benefit. For example: "Websites for tax offices that turn visitors into clients."

## 2. There's no clear call to action

Many sites never tell visitors what to do. Without a visible "Book," "Get a quote" or "Call now" button, an interested person can't find the way and leaves.

**The fix:** one main button, repeated at key points, with specific action text. Fewer options, more clarity.

## 3. It loads slowly

If your page takes more than three seconds, you lose visitors before they see anything. On phones it's even worse, and that's where most of your traffic is.

**The fix:** optimized images, lightweight code and decent hosting. ## 4. It doesn't build trust

People don't buy from a site that looks improvised. No reviews, no real photos, no clear contact details, and visitors hesitate.

**The fix:** add customer reviews, results, real photos and a visible contact section. Trust is built with proof.

## 5. It isn't built for phones

More than half your visitors come from a phone. If the site looks cramped or buttons are hard to tap, those customers are gone.

**The fix:** genuinely responsive design, tested on phones, with readable text and big buttons. More in [what responsive web design really means](/en/blog/responsive-web-design/).

## 6. Nobody finds it (no SEO)

A beautiful site that doesn't show up on Google is a store with no sign on a street with no foot traffic.

**The fix:** optimize titles, descriptions and content around the words your customers search, and work on your local presence. See [how to show up on Google Maps](/en/blog/how-to-show-up-on-google-maps/) and [SEO in the age of AI search](/en/blog/ai-search-seo-chatgpt-google/).

## 7. It talks about you, not your customer

The most common mistake: the site brags "we're leaders, ten years of experience, our mission…" when the customer only wants to know **what problem you'll solve for them.**

**The fix:** rewrite the content around the customer: their pain, their goals and how your service gets them there. The most important word on your site is "you," not "we."

## How to find your problem

You don't have to guess. Ask: can people tell what I do in five seconds? Is there an obvious button to contact me? Does it load fast on a phone? Does it build trust? Do I show up on Google? Every "no" is a leak you can plug. The full checklist is in [the 7 elements of a website that sells](/en/blog/website-elements-that-sell/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why isn't my website getting visitors?**
Almost always a lack of SEO and Google presence (Business Profile, keywords, content). No visibility, no traffic; no traffic, no sales.

**Does having a website guarantee more customers?**
Not on its own. A site sells when it's designed to convert: clear message, trust, speed and an obvious action.

**How long until improvements show?**
Conversion changes (message, buttons, speed) show almost immediately. SEO takes longer: weeks to a few months depending on competition.
