If your business goes to the customer (remodeling, roofing, plumbing, HVAC, cleaning, pest control, towing, mobile services), you eventually hit the same question: I cover five cities and three counties, so why do I only show up in mine?

The short answer nobody likes, but it saves months and money: **the service area you set in Google doesn't expand where you rank.** It informs; it doesn't position. Showing up in the next county over is earned with other pieces, and this guide covers all of them, in order.

## What Google actually says

A service area business is one that serves customers at the customer's location. These are the official rules:

- **You can add up to 20 service areas.**
- They're defined **by city, ZIP code or region**, not by drawing a radius.
- Service area boundaries **shouldn't extend more than about two hours of driving time** from where your business is based, per [Google's guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481).
- If you don't serve customers at your address, **hide it.** Google's own example is a plumber working from home.
- **No virtual offices or P.O. boxes.** A rented address where you don't operate can't have a profile.
- **One profile.** A service area business gets one profile for its central location, not one per city.
- **No cities or keywords in the business name.** "Ramirez Roofing" is fine; "Ramirez Roofing Houston Katy Cypress" isn't.

Those last two cause the most suspensions. Creating five profiles, one per city, isn't an aggressive strategy. It's the fastest way to lose the profile you already had.

## The misunderstanding that costs months

Here's the mistake I see in most cases: the owner adds twenty cities to the service area, waits a month, and doesn't appear in any new one.

Google says local results are ranked on three factors: **relevance, distance and prominence.** Distance is measured from your verified location, not from the list of cities you typed. Local SEO firms that have tested this on their own profiles report the same thing: rankings follow the verified address, and the service area setting is essentially informational.

Practical takeaway: set your service areas so people know how far you travel and so you don't get calls from outside your range. Just don't expect them to move your ranking. What moves it is below.

## What actually gets you found in the next county

### 1. A page per city or area on your own website

This is the most important piece and the only one you fully control. Your profile competes for the map; your website competes in the text results, and that's where you can cover territory.

The rule: **every page has to say something that only applies to that area.** The kind of work done there, common problems, permit timelines, neighborhoods covered, real job photos and reviews from that area.

Twenty identical pages with the city name swapped are doorway pages; Google ignores or penalizes them, and they don't persuade anyone. If you don't have real material for a city yet, don't build its page yet. Build it after your first job there.

### 2. Reviews that mention the service and the city

"They replaced our roof in Katy" is worth far more than "great service." Ask the day you finish, send a direct link, and reply to all of them.

### 3. Fresh photos and posts

A profile with new job photos every month moves; one with four photos from two years ago doesn't. Ten minutes a week, free.

### 4. The right primary category and every service listed

Category is the strongest relevance signal. List services in the words customers use: "roof leak repair," "bathroom remodel," "water heater installation."

### 5. Local mentions and links

Chambers of commerce, trade associations, county directories, local news, suppliers who list installers. Slow, and it's what separates a new profile from an established one.

## If your pin is outside the county you want

This is the hard case: **if your verified address is an hour from the county you want to work in, that map will be an uphill battle** no matter what you do with the service area. Your real options:

1. **Win in the text results** with city-and-service pages on your website.
2. **Have a real presence there:** a shop, a yard or an office where you actually operate, and verify that location.
3. **Buy traffic meanwhile** with search ads targeted to that county and a landing page built for it.

Inventing addresses is not an option. A suspension leaves you without a profile and without the reviews it took two years to collect.

## Texas: the five-county metro

A Houston contractor doesn't really work in Houston; they work across the metro, split among **Harris, Fort Bend, Montgomery, Brazoria and Galveston** counties.

- **Distances are measured in traffic, not miles.** The two-hour rule runs out inside the Houston metro itself. List the cities you actually serve.
- **Prioritize the suburbs.** Katy, Cypress, Pearland, Spring, Sugar Land: less competition for the same search and often better tickets.
- **Service plus city is the search that buys.** "Roof repair Katy" has less volume than the generic term and much more intent.
- **Bilingual matters more here than almost anywhere.** The same business serves one customer in English and the next in Spanish, so city pages are worth having in both languages.

## Florida: the tri-county area and the season

In South Florida the real market is three adjacent counties: **Miami-Dade, Broward and Palm Beach.**

- **You'll cross county lines every day.** Here it does make sense to list all three as service areas and to have a page for each.
- **Hurricane season, June 1 to November 30,** sets the calendar for every trade tied to water and roofs. Repair searches spike on specific days, the worst time to discover your site can't handle the traffic or reply on its own.
- **The state license is a selling point.** Florida customers are used to checking it; put it in plain view.

## Checklist to get it working this week

1. Decide whether you're a service area business. If nobody comes to your address, **hide it.**
2. List only the areas you really serve, within two hours' drive, up to 20.
3. Make sure the business name has **no added cities or keywords.**
4. Find and remove duplicate profiles of your own business.
5. Pick the exact primary category and list every service.
6. Upload real job photos weekly.
7. Ask for reviews the day you finish, with a direct link, and reply to all.
8. Write one page per city **with unique content,** starting with your top three.
9. Link those pages from your menu and your profile.
10. Track calls and messages by source, so you know which county is actually paying the bills.

## How long it takes

Three to six months for real movement in local SEO. What moves in weeks is the profile itself: category, photos, services and reviews send signals fast. Anyone promising page one in thirty days across five counties is selling smoke.
