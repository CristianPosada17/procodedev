Yes, you can **create a free website for your small business**, and in some cases it's the right call. What almost nobody explains is where the line sits between "free and good enough" and "free and costing you customers," or what a site needs, free or paid, to bring in work instead of just existing.

This guide covers both. The technical side (domain, hosting, platform, HTML) is in the [step-by-step guide to building a website](/en/blog/how-to-build-a-website/).

## The options that are actually free

### Google Business Profile

Truly free and, for a local business, **often more valuable in year one than any website**. You show up on Google Maps with your phone number, hours, photos and reviews, and people call straight from it.

If you only do one thing this week, complete it: real photos, correct hours, service area, and a review request to each of your last five customers. Step by step in [how to show up on Google Maps](/en/blog/how-to-show-up-on-google-maps/).

### Free plans from Wix, WordPress.com or Webflow

They work, with three conditions:

- The address carries their brand: `yourbusiness.wixsite.com/...`. On a business card, it shows.
- They display their ads on your site.
- They limit storage and pages.

The full comparison is in [the best website builders compared](/en/blog/best-website-builders/).

### GitHub Pages

Free, no ads, no third-party branding, but you build the site in HTML. That path is explained in the HTML section of [the step-by-step guide](/en/blog/how-to-build-a-website/).

### Carrd, Linktree and similar

Free for a single, very simple page. A bridge while you build something better, especially if you sell through Instagram.

## What is never free

- **The domain.** $12–$20 a year (<a href="https://www.hostinger.com/?REFERRALCODE=CristianDev" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener" target="_blank">Hostinger</a> often includes the first year with hosting). It's the first thing worth paying for: `yourbusiness.com` instead of `yourbusiness.wixsite.com` changes how people see you, and it stays yours no matter how many times you switch platforms.
- **Your time.** Twenty or thirty hours building a site aren't free. At $25 an hour, that "free" site cost $600.
- **The sales you don't make.** A slow site that doesn't build trust loses customers who had already decided. That cost never shows up on an invoice.

## Free or paid, the site has the same job

Whether it's a free plan or a custom build, a business website is judged by one thing: does someone who already wanted to hire you get in touch, or leave with doubts? These decisions determine that, and none of them depends on the platform.

### Decision 1: what job will the site do

A site can do three different jobs, and almost none does all three well:

- **Close.** Someone already knows you (a referral, your truck, Instagram) and visits to confirm you're real before reaching out. The most profitable job and the most ignored.
- **Attract.** People who don't know you find you by searching for your service on Google. It takes months and needs several pages with content.
- **Convert paid traffic.** Visitors from ads who must be convinced in a single screen. That's what a [landing page](/en/services/landing-pages/) is for.

Most service businesses need the first one urgently and the second in the medium term.

### Decision 2: what people see in the first five seconds

Three facts without scrolling: **what you do, for whom, and where.** "Innovative solutions for your growth" says nothing. "Residential plumbing in Houston, same-day emergencies" says everything.

### Decision 3: one page per service

If you sell three services, you need three pages, not a list of three bullets. Each service is searched with different words, and a page about everything ranks for nothing. This is where free plans fall short first: they limit pages exactly when you need more.

### Decision 4: proof people can verify

- Photos of your real work, with place and date.
- Reviews with full names, ideally linked to Google.
- Your face and your name.
- License, insurance or certifications, if your trade has them.
- A service area named by city, not "nationwide."

No made-up round numbers: "500+ happy customers" costs credibility.

### Decision 5: contact where the thumb is

Phone and text visible in the header, and a sticky button on mobile. A three-field form: name, phone and what they need. To audit your current site, use [the 7 elements of a website that sells](/en/blog/website-elements-that-sell/).

## When free is the right call

- You're validating a business that doesn't earn yet.
- You need something this week so you don't miss a specific opportunity.
- You sell through social media and just need a place with your services and contact info.
- You have more time than money, which is completely legitimate.

## When free starts getting expensive

- A customer told you they couldn't find your site, or that it looked off on their phone.
- You're paying for ads that land on a page showing someone else's ads.
- You sell services over $500: the gap between what you charge and how you look hurts.
- You need to show up on Google for your service, not just your name.

## The five-year numbers

| | Free + domain | Paid builder | Custom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | ~$20 | ~$250 | ~$450 |
| 5-year total | ~$100 | ~$1,250 | ~$700 |
| Third-party ads | Yes | No | No |
| You can take it with you | No | No | Yes |

The point isn't that free is bad. It's that **the paid builder is the most expensive option over time**, and it's the middle path almost everyone picks by default.

## How much to invest at each stage

- **Just starting, no steady clients:** free options plus the domain and your own weekend.
- **Established business, the site closes sales:** $350–$1,000 one-time. It pays for itself with one or two jobs.
- **The site is your main lead source:** $900–$2,500, plus a monthly budget to bring traffic.

Always compare it against what a customer is worth to you. What moves the number is in [how much a small business website costs](/en/blog/small-business-website-cost/).

## How to know if it's working

Don't watch visits. Track three things:

1. **Contacts:** calls, texts and form submissions from the site.
2. **Where they come from:** Google, social, ads or people who already knew you.
3. **How many became customers.**

If the site gets 400 visits a month and no messages, the site is the problem, and the usual causes are in [why your website isn't getting leads](/en/blog/why-your-website-isnt-getting-leads/). If it gets 40 visits and two messages, the site works and what's missing is traffic, which is a job for [digital marketing](/en/services/digital-marketing/) and [SEO](/en/services/seo/).

## A sensible plan if you're starting with no budget

1. **This week:** a complete Google Business Profile, with photos and five reviews requested by hand.
2. **This month:** buy the domain and set up a free page, even a simple one, with your services, area and contact info.
3. **Once revenue is steady:** move to your own site with a page per service and point the same domain at it. You lose nothing.
