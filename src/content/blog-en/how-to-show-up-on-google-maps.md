When someone searches "dentist near me" or "plumber in Dallas," Google first shows a map with three businesses. Showing up there is one of the most profitable things a local business can do: these are customers **already looking for you and ready to buy.** This guide explains, without jargon, how to show up on Google Maps and climb in local searches.

## The short answer

To show up on Google Maps you need to create and verify your **Google Business Profile** (formerly Google My Business), complete it fully, earn reviews and keep it active. It's free, and it's step one of local SEO for any business with customers in a physical area.

## What Google Business Profile is

It's your business listing on Google Search and Maps: name, address or service area, hours, phone, photos, reviews and buttons to call or visit your website. When someone searches for a service in your area, Google decides who appears on the map based on these profiles.

## How to set up your profile, step by step

1. **Go to google.com/business** and sign in with your Google account.
2. **Enter your business name.** If a listing already exists, claim it; if not, create one.
3. **Choose the right primary category** (for example "Dentist" or "Roofing contractor"). This is key: Google shows you based on category.
4. **Add your address or service area.** If customers come to you, add the address; if you go to them, set your service area and hide the address.
5. **Add your phone number and website.** This is where you connect your site to receive visits and inquiries.
6. **Verify the business.** Google verifies by phone, email, video or mail depending on the case. Unverified, you don't show up.

## How to climb on the map

Creating the profile gets you in the game; reaching the top three takes work:

- **Complete everything:** hours, services, description, attributes. Complete profiles rank better.
- **Upload real photos regularly:** storefront, interior, team, finished work. Google rewards active profiles.
- **Earn reviews and reply to all of them:** reviews are among the strongest local ranking factors. Ask happy customers with a direct link, and reply politely, even to negative ones.
- **Keep your details consistent (NAP):** name, address and phone identical on your website, social profiles and directories.
- **Post updates:** offers, news, recent jobs. A living profile sends good signals.
- **Connect a solid website:** Google trusts businesses with a fast site of their own that matches the listing.

If you serve several cities or counties, read [how service areas really work](/en/blog/google-business-profile-service-area/): adding cities to your profile doesn't make you rank in them.

## The mistake almost everyone makes

Creating the profile, verifying it… and abandoning it. A profile with no new reviews, photos or updates slides down over time. Local SEO isn't "set it and forget it"; it's keeping the profile active month after month.

## Your profile and your website: better together

The profile makes you visible; **your website converts.** Many customers find you on the map, click through to your site to see if you seem trustworthy, and decide there. If your site is slow or doesn't sell, you lose the customer Google sent you. ## Frequently asked questions

**How much does it cost to show up on Google Maps?**
Creating and verifying a Google Business Profile is free. You can improve results with SEO and a good website, but the profile itself costs nothing.

**How long until my business appears?**
After verification the listing usually shows within days. Reaching the top three depends on reviews, activity and competition: typically weeks to several months.

**Do I need a storefront to appear on Maps?**
No. If you go to customers, you can set a service area without showing your exact address.

**Do reviews really matter?**
A lot. Review count, rating and frequency are among the most influential local SEO factors, and they persuade people who are on the fence.
